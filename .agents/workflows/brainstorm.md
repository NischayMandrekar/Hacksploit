---
description: "Generate genuinely distinct approaches or ideas before judging any of them. Usage: /brainstorm <problem or question>"
---

Divergent thinking, then convergent — kept as two separate phases,
because judging ideas while generating them quietly narrows generation
down to "safe" variations of the first idea.

## Phase 1 — Divergent (generate, don't judge)

1. Generate several genuinely different approaches, not variations on one
   idea with the knobs turned differently. If two "approaches" would end
   up looking similar in practice, they're one idea, not two.
2. Defer judgment completely during this phase — do not caveat, rank, or
   dismiss an idea while listing it. A weird or currently-impractical idea
   stays in the list; it can be cut in Phase 2, not before it's even
   written down.
3. Include at least one approach that deliberately breaks an assumption
   the "obvious" approach takes for granted (different architecture,
   different tradeoff entirely, not just a different library).
4. Aim for quantity over polish in this phase — a rough list of distinct
   directions beats one fully fleshed-out idea.

## Phase 2 — Convergent (now judge)

5. Only after Phase 1 is complete, evaluate: what does each approach cost,
   what does it assume, where does it break down, what's it actually good
   for.
6. State a recommendation if asked, but keep the reasoning visible —
   which tradeoff you're prioritizing and why — rather than presenting
   the pick as obviously correct.
7. Don't silently drop the ideas that lost — a brief one-line note on why
   each alternative wasn't picked is worth more than deleting them from
   the conversation.
